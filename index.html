Choosing your structure

Pick the right entity for your goals

Most Malaysian businesses register with the Companies Commission of Malaysia (SSM). The structure you choose shapes your liability, credibility, and cost — here's a quick guide, and we'll help you decide.

Private Limited Sdn Bhd

The most popular entity in Malaysia — a private company limited by shares, and a separate legal entity from its owners. Built for growth, credibility, and structure.

  • Limited liability — personal assets are protected
  • Strong credibility with banks, clients & investors
  • Perpetual succession; shares transfer easily
  • Easier access to funding

Best for: growing businesses, startups seeking investment, and multi-owner companies.

Requires at least 1 shareholder and 1 director ordinarily resident in Malaysia.

Public Limited Bhd

A public company limited by shares — shares can be offered to the public and listed on Bursa Malaysia. Suited to large-scale expansion and major capital raising.

  • Raise substantial capital from the public
  • Enhanced reputation and transparency
  • Easier mergers, acquisitions & alliances
  • Constant, transparent market valuation

Best for: large corporations and businesses with national or international ambitions.

Requires at least 2 directors resident in Malaysia; no maximum shareholders.

Company Limited by Guarantee CLBG

Set up for charitable, educational, religious, or other non-profit purposes — to advance a cause, not to distribute profit. Has members instead of shareholders.

  • No share capital — members, not shareholders
  • Liability limited to a small guaranteed amount
  • All income applied to its stated objects
  • Governed by a Constitution

Best for: charities, foundations, professional bodies, and non-profits.

Profits cannot be distributed to members; a Constitution is required.

Limited Liability Partnership PLT / LLP

A body corporate and separate legal entity — an alternative to a conventional partnership or a Sdn Bhd, combining limited liability with partnership flexibility.

  • Limited liability for each partner
  • Separate legal entity with perpetual succession
  • Flexible management via a Partnership Agreement
  • Lower compliance costs than a company

Best for: professional firms, joint ventures, and SMEs wanting flexibility.

Requires at least 2 partners and 1 compliance officer.

Setup & compliance guide

Setting up and staying compliant

Doing business in Malaysia involves statutory requirements and licences. Here's a practical guide to the most common ones — we can assist with all of them. New to this? See our step-by-step guide to registering a company in Malaysia, or if you're overseas, our guide for foreign founders.

To incorporate a private limited company with SSM under the Companies Act 2016, you'll need:

  • Director — at least one, aged 18+, ordinarily resident in Malaysia, not a bankrupt or recently convicted of certain offences.
  • Shareholder — at least one (individual or corporate). In most sectors, 100% foreign ownership is allowed.
  • Paid-up capital — no legal minimum; a company can start from as little as RM1 (higher may be needed for licences, work passes, or banking).
  • Company name — searched and reserved with SSM before incorporation.
  • Registered office — a Malaysian address where statutory records are kept (commonly the company secretary's office).
  • Company secretary — a licensed secretary appointed within 30 days of incorporation.
  • Other particulars — nature of business, director & shareholder details, and the SSM fee. A constitution is optional for most companies.

Every company in Malaysia must have at least one company secretary — a statutory requirement under the Companies Act 2016. The secretary must be:

  • A natural person, aged 18 or above.
  • A Malaysian citizen or permanent resident ordinarily residing in Malaysia.
  • A member of a prescribed professional body (e.g. MAICSA, MIA, MACS, or the Malaysian Bar) or an individual licensed by SSM.

The first secretary must be appointed within 30 days of incorporation, and the office may not stay vacant for more than 30 days. The secretary handles statutory filings, maintains registers and minutes, and advises the board on compliance. This is our core service at Ghinex.

Foreign nationals need a valid pass to work in Malaysia. The employer must generally first register with the Immigration Department's Expatriate Services Division (ESD) and have its company profile approved; some sectors also require clearance from the relevant regulator before passes are issued.

Employment Pass (EP)

For expatriates in managerial, executive, or technical roles. There are three categories by basic monthly salary (rates effective 1 June 2026; allowances and bonuses are excluded):

CategoryBasic monthly salaryKey features
Category IRM20,000 and aboveSenior management & key posts; renewable up to 10 years with the same employer; dependants & a domestic helper allowed
Category IIRM10,000 – RM19,999Managerial & professional roles; renewable up to 10 years; dependants allowed
Category IIIRM5,000 – RM9,999Skilled/executive roles; up to 5 years (RM7,000+ for manufacturing); limited dependant rights

Dependants (spouse and children under 18) may accompany Category I and II holders. Once the company and the post are approved, an EP application is typically processed within a few weeks.

Other common passes

  • Professional Visit Pass (PVP) — for foreign experts providing short-term services (training, installation, consulting) while remaining employed by their overseas company; valid up to 12 months.
  • Temporary Employment Visit Pass — for foreign workers in approved sectors (manufacturing, construction, plantation, agriculture, selected services), subject to quota and source-country conditions.
  • Dependant Pass — for the spouse and children of eligible EP holders; a Long-Term Social Visit Pass can cover parents or a common-law spouse.
  • Residence Pass-Talent (RP-T) — up to 10 years for highly qualified expatriates who meet the criteria, with greater freedom to change employers.

For distributive-trade businesses, a WRT licence is usually a prerequisite before EPs are granted. We handle ESD registration, the company and post applications, and each individual pass end to end.

Businesses operating from physical premises generally need two licences from the local council (Pihak Berkuasa Tempatan) where the premises sit — for example DBKL in Kuala Lumpur, MBPJ in Petaling Jaya, or MBSA in Shah Alam.

Business premises licence

  • Your licence to operate at that address — required before you begin trading.
  • Typical documents: SSM registration, tenancy agreement or proof of ownership, a floor/layout plan, and photos of the premises.
  • Regulated activities — food & beverage, childcare, clinics, entertainment — carry extra conditions and inspections, and may need support letters from the Fire & Rescue Department (BOMBA) or Health Department.

Signboard / advertisement licence

  • Required before displaying any signboard or advertisement at the premises.
  • You submit the signboard design showing its size, wording, colours, materials and exact location.
  • Bahasa Malaysia must be given prominence; other languages may appear alongside. Councils also set rules on dimensions, placement, and sometimes permitted colours.

Both are usually valid for one year and renewed annually; fees vary with the council, premises size, and signboard dimensions. We prepare the applications and liaise with the council on your behalf.

The Wholesale, Retail and Trade (WRT) licence is required for foreign-owned companies before they carry on distributive trade in Malaysia — wholesale, retail, franchising, direct selling, import/export trading, and certain restaurant and service businesses.

  • Authority — Ministry of Domestic Trade and Cost of Living (KPDN).
  • Who needs it — companies with foreign equity carrying on distributive trade; it is generally a prerequisite before such a company can obtain Employment Passes in this sector.
  • Capital — minimum RM1,000,000 paid-up capital for most categories; substantially higher thresholds and extra conditions apply to large formats such as department stores, superstores, and hypermarkets (which may also require Bumiputera participation).
  • Premises — a physical business premises in a commercially-zoned location with a tenancy of at least 12 months; virtual offices are not accepted.
  • Validity & restrictions — generally 1–2 years and renewable. Certain sub-sectors (mini-markets, convenience stores, fuel stations, and some personal services) are closed or restricted to foreign participation.

We assess whether your activities require a WRT licence, structure the application to meet the conditions, and follow it through with KPDN.

Manufacturers may need a Manufacturing Licence (ML) under the Industrial Coordination Act 1975, approved by the Ministry of Investment, Trade and Industry (MITI) and processed through the Malaysian Investment Development Authority (MIDA).

  • Who needs it — manufacturing companies with shareholders' funds of RM2.5 million and above, or engaging 75 or more full-time employees. Smaller operations are exempt, but can apply to MIDA for a confirmation letter of exemption (often needed for banking, incentives, or expatriate posts).
  • What MIDA covers — as the one-stop agency for manufacturing and selected services investments, MIDA also handles tax incentives (Pioneer Status, Investment Tax Allowance), duty exemptions on machinery and raw materials, and approval of expatriate posts.
  • Conditions — approvals may carry conditions on equity, value-add, technology, and local participation, depending on the industry.

We prepare the manufacturing-licence application — along with any incentive or expatriate-post applications — and follow through with MIDA and MITI.

Companies in warehousing, logistics, and trading may need licences under the Customs Act 1967, issued by the Royal Malaysian Customs Department (JKDM). A bonded warehouse lets imported dutiable goods be stored with duty and taxes suspended until release.

  • Public bonded warehouse — for storing third-party goods; ~RM1,000,000 minimum paid-up capital and a substantial storage area.
  • Private bonded warehouse — for storing your own goods; ~RM150,000 minimum paid-up capital.
  • Licensed Manufacturing Warehouse (LMW) — for export-oriented manufacturers; duty exemptions on imported inputs and machinery.

We advise on the current requirements as part of our licensing support service.

Licence conditions, fees, and salary thresholds in this guide are as at July 2026 and are subject to change by the relevant authorities. Contact us for the latest requirements for your specific case.

Governance & answers

Policies & frequently asked questions

Trust is the foundation of everything we do — here's how we work, and answers to what founders ask most.

Is a company secretary mandatory in Malaysia?
Yes. Under the Companies Act 2016, every company must appoint at least one qualified company secretary within 30 days of incorporation. Ghinex can act as your named secretary.
How long does it take to incorporate a Sdn Bhd?
Once your proposed name is approved and the documents are signed, registration with SSM is typically completed within a few working days.
Who can set up a Sdn Bhd?
At least one director ordinarily resident in Malaysia and at least one shareholder (they may be the same person). Paid-up capital can start from as little as RM1.
Can foreigners own a Malaysian company?
For most business activities, 100% foreign ownership is permitted; some regulated sectors carry conditions. We advise on structure, licensing, and opening a corporate bank account.
What ongoing compliance will my company have?
Maintaining statutory registers, lodging the annual return, preparing (and where required auditing) financial statements, and keeping SSM filings up to date. We manage this for you.
How are your fees structured?
For common needs like foreign market entry, we publish fixed-fee packages up front. Other engagements are tailored — tell us your plans and we'll prepare a clear quote, no obligation.
